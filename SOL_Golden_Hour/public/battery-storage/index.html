Purpose before product

Identify whether your priority is supporting critical loads, using stored solar, managing peaks or investigating an infrastructure opportunity.

Power & energy

kW and MW describe power: the rate of delivery. kWh and MWh describe stored energy. Both matter when evaluating loads and duration.

The complete system

Switchgear, protection, controls, utility rules, location and the electrical design affect how a battery can operate. A battery alone does not guarantee islanding.

A battery decision begins with an operating scenario.

Describe the loads or process you want to support. For backup, distinguish essential circuits from the entire property and share a desired duration if you know it. For commercial use, explain the operational objective; tariff and load data can come later.

Existing solar can influence the options, but compatibility and operating strategy require assessment. Commissioning, safety requirements, permits, controls, service and maintenance should form part of any proposed solution. Supplier and project-specific terms need confirmation before a commitment.

If you have a specification, use the appropriate units. If you do not, leave the optional power and energy fields blank. “Unknown” is a useful starting point, and avoids false precision. A qualified technical review must establish an appropriate configuration.

Before you begin.

What is the difference between kW and kWh?

kW is a rate of power; kWh is an amount of energy. The same distinction applies to MW and MWh at larger scale.

Does every battery provide backup?

No. Backup depends on equipment configuration, controls, electrical design and approvals. The intended behavior must be verified.

Can storage be considered on its own?

Yes. Existing solar is one of the initial questions; new solar is not a requirement for an inquiry.
