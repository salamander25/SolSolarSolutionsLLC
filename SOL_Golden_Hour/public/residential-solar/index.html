Start with what matters

A review built around your need.

Homeowners, authorized property representatives and people planning a home energy upgrade.

Start with your home

Location, roof condition, shade and property authority shape the first review. An attractive roof is a starting point; it is not a production estimate.

Make room for real life

Your electric usage and future loads matter. An EV, home business or changing occupancy can influence what should be evaluated. An approximate range is enough to begin.

Decide what backup means

If storage interests you, identify essential loads and the duration you have in mind. Whole-home backup and automatic operation depend on the complete system design.

Know what belongs in the next conversation.

The first step is to understand the property and check available installation and service coverage. If there appears to be a fit, the next stage can address roof suitability, electrical equipment, utility rules and your usage. We will explain what information is needed before you share detailed documents.

Solar production, utility-bill effects, financing terms and available programs all depend on your circumstances. A useful comparison identifies the system assumptions, current utility tariff, costs, ownership terms and responsibilities. Any savings or incentive discussion should be supported by current project-specific information.

You do not need to know the right system size. Choose “help me determine this” and describe the outcome you want. Keep utility account numbers, passwords, tax records and financial credentials out of the first inquiry.

Before you begin.

Do I need my electric bill now?

No. A rough usage range is optional. Detailed usage can be requested later through an appropriate sharing process.

Can solar keep my home running during an outage?

Solar alone does not establish backup capability. Storage, controls, electrical design and utility requirements must be assessed for the intended loads.

Will I receive a price or incentive approval from this form?

No. This is an inquiry. Firm pricing, financing and program eligibility need a separate verified review.
