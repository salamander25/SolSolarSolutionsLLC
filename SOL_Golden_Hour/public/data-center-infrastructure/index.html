Start with what matters

A review built around your need.

Developers, landowners, authorized advisors and infrastructure stakeholders evaluating prospective data-center sites or power strategies.

Define the requirement

Initial and ultimate power needs are different. State each in MW, if known, and distinguish a desired date from a utility-confirmed delivery date.

Check the basis

Utility correspondence, formal studies, site control and development status help separate an early prospect from a supported opportunity.

Coordinate the next review

Establish roles, information-sharing boundaries and the specialists needed to review power, land and technical constraints.

A serious infrastructure conversation starts with specific questions.

A large parcel and nearby power lines do not make a shovel-ready data-center site. Early screening should distinguish observed facts, documented utility evidence and assumptions. Begin with nonconfidential information and the decision you are trying to make.

Sol acts as an origination, qualification, business-development and project-coordination contact. Sol is not offering cloud hosting, operating a data center, guaranteeing power delivery or representing unverified land as powered. Engineering, construction, financing and operating responsibilities are project-specific.

Fiber, cooling, electrical architecture, critical systems, site drawings and utility documents belong in a later secure due-diligence process. Solar and storage may form part of a strategy, but do not automatically satisfy a data center’s reliability, capacity or delivery-date requirements.
